# Foreign-born Homicides in the US (2014-2024)
## NVSS Detailed Mortality - Limited Geography Files

### Project Summary
**Objective:** This project measures homicide victimization rates for native-born and foreign-born residents of the United States from 2014 through 2024.

**Study Population:** All resident deaths in the 50 states and the District of Columbia for data years 2014-2024 with an underlying cause of death classified as assault (homicide): ICD-10 codes X85-Y09 and Y87.1, corresponding to codes 128 and 129 of the NCHS 113 Selected Causes of Death recode. No restriction is placed on age, sex, race, or ethnicity. Decedents will be classified as native-born or foreign-born using the state/country of birth recode, following U.S. Census Bureau convention: persons born in the 50 states, the District of Columbia, or a U.S. territory are classified as native, and persons born outside the United States and its territories are classified as foreign-born. The geographic level of analysis is the state, based on state of residence.

**Analytical Methods:** The analysis is descriptive. Homicide deaths will be tabulated by nativity, year, sex, and five-year age group, and divided by corresponding population denominators from the U.S. Census Bureau's American Community Survey to produce homicide rates per 100,000 population. Denominators will use the ACS nativity classification so that numerator and denominator definitions correspond. Because the native and foreign-born populations differ substantially in age structure, rates will be age-adjusted by direct standardization to the 2000 U.S. standard population, and age-specific rates will also be reported. Where cell sizes permit, rates will be presented overall by sex, year, and state. All counts, rates, and percentages based on fewer than ten observations are supressed in accordance with the NCHS data suppression standard.

**Project Significance:** Immigration and violence policy debates are frequently conducted without reference to rigorous evidence on who is actually at elevated risk of violent death. Establishing an accurate, nationally complete account of homicide victimization by nativity serves the public health objective of directing violence-prevention resources toward the populations at greatest risk. Prior research on this question has been limited to single years and partial state coverage; an eleven-year national series permits assessment of whether observed differentials are stable or changing, including across the substantial national increase in homicide during 2020-2021 and the subsequent decline. This purpose carries a particular urgency, given the current immigration policy landscape. As enforcement priorities intensify and public debate over immigration grows, discussions of immigrants and violence are too often shaped by anecdote and rhetoric. Data-driven evidence is therefore essential to protecting vulnerable populations.
